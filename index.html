B‑CAUSE

Block-wise Causal Exploration for Subject-driven Video Generation

In diffusion transformers, appearance and motion are entangled inside shared attention blocks — so naive customization corrupts the motion prior. B‑CAUSE causally traces which blocks encode identity versus dynamics, then fine-tunes appearance blocks for identity while steering motion blocks at inference to keep motion natural.

The problem

Customizing a DiT video model breaks its motion

Subject customization was built for U-Net video models. It breaks on diffusion transformers like CogVideoX — and the reason is simply where appearance and motion live.

U‑Net prior methods

Appearance and motion sit in separate attention modules. Fine-tune the appearance one, and motion is never touched.

✓ motion preserved
DiT CogVideoX

Appearance and motion are entangled in one shared block. A LoRA that learns the subject rewrites motion with it.

✗ motion breaks — the video stiffens or drifts

So the question becomes: which blocks carry appearance versus motion — and can that split be measured and put to use?

Causal tracing

What each block is responsible for

Corrupt the prompt, then restore one block at a time. How much appearance and motion return is that block’s causal contribution.

Each restored clip is scored against the clean reference on every frame by a weighted panel of seven complementary metrics.

Method

A dual-phase strategy that follows the causal map

The map hands us four block groups. We use them to split customization into a training phase that touches only identity and an inference phase that restores dynamics — never fighting each other.

PHASE 1 · TRAIN

Fine-tune appearance blocks only

Lightweight LoRA adapters are placed on appearance-sensitive blocks (plus the shared core), trained with a masked reconstruction loss and a cross-attention loss to encode subject identity — leaving motion blocks frozen.

PHASE 2 · INFERENCE

Guide with motion blocks

At denoising time, within a noise-level window, we read the motion energy carried by the motion-sensitive blocks and compare it to per-block reference targets. When motion falls short, a guidance gradient nudges the latent toward those targets — restoring natural dynamics without disturbing the learned identity, at a strength calibrated per subject.
